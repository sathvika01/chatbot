A Beginner's Guide to Dragon Care
===================================

Dragons are magnificent companions, but they are not low-maintenance pets.
This guide covers the essentials: feeding, housing, exercise, and health.

Feeding
-------
An adult dragon eats roughly 4 kilograms of food per day. Their diet should be
about 70 percent roasted meat and 30 percent volcanic rock candy, which provides
the minerals they need for healthy scale growth. Never feed a dragon chocolate;
it interferes with their fire glands and can cause dangerous backfires.

Young dragons, called hatchlings, eat smaller meals five times a day. Their
favorite food is ember-roasted chicken. Hatchlings double in size every three
months during their first year, so expect your food bill to grow quickly.

Housing
-------
A dragon needs a den of at least 20 square meters, lined with heat-resistant
stone. The den temperature should stay between 35 and 45 degrees Celsius.
Dragons sleep 14 hours a day and are grumpy when woken early, so the den
should be in a quiet part of the house.

Exercise
--------
Dragons must fly at least one hour every day to stay healthy. A tired dragon
is a well-behaved dragon. The best time for flying is just after sunset, when
the air is cool. Always fly your dragon away from airports and populated areas.

Health
------
The most common dragon ailment is scale rot, caused by damp living conditions.
Keep the den dry and inspect the belly scales weekly. A healthy dragon's flame
burns blue at the base and orange at the tip. If the flame turns green, consult
a dragon veterinarian immediately, as this is a sign of fire-gland infection.

Grooming
--------
Brush your dragon's scales once a week with a brass-bristle brush, always
stroking from head to tail. Dragons shed their scales twice a year, in spring
and autumn. During shedding season, add extra volcanic rock candy to their
diet to support new scale growth.

Training
---------
Start training when your dragon is three months old. The three essential
commands are "perch", "gentle", and "no fire". Reward good behavior with a
piece of volcanic rock candy. Training sessions should last no more than
15 minutes, because young dragons have short attention spans.

Travel
------
When traveling with a dragon, you need a fireproof carrier and a permit from
the Bureau of Mythical Creatures. Dragons are not allowed on commercial
airlines, but most dragon owners simply fly alongside their dragon instead.
